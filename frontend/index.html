Density & zoning glossary

Density metrics
FAR — Floor Area Ratio. Buildable floor area ÷ lot area. FAR 3.0 on a 10,000 sf lot = 30,000 sf of building. The single biggest driver of how much you can build.
du/ac — Dwelling units per acre. Caps how many homes fit on the land regardless of size.
Lot coverage — % of the lot the building footprint may cover.
Height — Max in feet or stories; often the binding constraint near transit.
Density tiers (du/ac)
Low ≤7 single-family / large-lot
Medium 8–20 townhomes, duplex, small multifamily
High 21–60 mid-rise multifamily
Very high 60+ high-rise / urban core
Zoning code families
R / RS / RE / SF — Residential, single-family
RM / RD / MF / RH — Residential, multifamily (RM-high = densest)
C / CB / CC / CG / B — Commercial / business
MU / MX / MXD — Mixed-use (residential over retail)
M / I / IL / IH — Industrial / manufacturing (light → heavy)
O / OR — Office / Office-residential
OS / P / REC — Open space / parks / recreation
A / AG / AR — Agricultural / rural
D / DT / DC / DX — Downtown / downtown-core / downtown-mixed
T3 → T6 — Form-based "Transect" (Miami 21, SmartCode): T3 sub-urban → T4 general urban → T5 urban center → T6 urban core (densest). Suffix shows max stories, e.g. T6-48 = 48 stories.
PUD / PD / PDD — Planned (Unit) Development — custom negotiated standards, not a fixed table.
Codes vary by city. Click any parcel for that jurisdiction's exact definition, standards and bonus provisions via the code link.

How the condo screen works

The statute. FS 718.117 permits termination of a condominium on 80% approval with no more than 5% objecting. Many older declarations set a higher bar, frequently unanimity.
Kaufman language. Whether the statutory 80% reaches a given building turns on whether its declaration adopts the Condominium Act as amended from time to time — after Kaufman v. Shere, 347 So. 2d 627 (Fla. 3d DCA 1977). Nobody publishes which buildings have it, which is why the screen is two stages: rank 6,160 buildings from free bulk data, then read declarations only for the survivors.
Original vs by amendment. Florida's Third DCA ruled against a developer that acquired 183 of 192 units and then amended the declaration to insert Kaufman language and drop the threshold from unanimous to 80%. Kaufman in the original recorded declaration carries weight; Kaufman added later by a bulk owner is the fact pattern that lost. The two are recorded as separate findings and never collapsed.
The score weights ownership concentration highest, because a single owner above about 20% means someone is already assembling the building. Age, scale and absentee share make up the rest.
Sale prices. The tax roll stamps an instrument's whole consideration on every folio it conveys, so a deed covering ten units shows the package price ten times. Comps here group sales into instruments and divide package prices down to a per-unit basis; single-unit sales are reported separately, because those are the arm's-length signal.
